UI: Card — Recipe App

Investigation. Can the schema.org Recipe card grow into a small kitchen app — like the standalone example at recipe.stoumann.dk — without leaving the card system? Clicking the card below opens a fullscreen popover overlay (zero-JS open/close via invoker commands, same pattern as the lightbox). The steps accordion from schema.html is replaced by a button that opens a cook-mode popover: one large readable step at a time, Screen Wake Lock with a live indicator, read-aloud via the Speech API and via a pre-recorded audio file, and hands-free voice navigation. Findings at the bottom. Nothing in the existing schema demo files changed.

    Classic Spaghetti Carbonara

    Step 1 of 6

    1. Boil & salt

      Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil, then salt it generously — it should taste like the sea.

    2. Crisp the guanciale

      Cut the guanciale into short strips and crisp them in a dry pan over medium heat until golden. Pull the pan off the heat.

    3. Make the cream

      Whisk the egg yolks with the grated pecorino and plenty of coarsely ground black pepper into a thick paste.

    4. Cook the pasta

      Cook the spaghetti until just shy of al dente. Scoop out a cup of the starchy cooking water before you drain.

    5. Toss, off the heat

      Toss the hot pasta with the guanciale and its fat. Off the heat, stir in the egg mixture, loosening with splashes of pasta water until glossy.

    6. Serve

      Serve immediately in warm bowls, with more pecorino and black pepper on top.

    Findings

    What needs zero JavaScript

    • Open/close of both layers. The card's cover button and the steps button are plain invokers — command="show-popover" commandfor="…". The platform handles the top layer, Esc, light-dismiss, focus and ::backdrop; same mechanism as the media lightbox.
    • Stacking. Cook mode is a sibling of the app popover — a popover inside a closed (display: none) popover can never render, and the card's own steps button must open cook mode standalone. Opened from inside the app, the invoker relationship makes the app its ancestor in the popover stack, so it stays open underneath. (popovertarget= is the wider-support spelling of the same invoker; this page follows the repo's command= convention, with a JS fallback.)
    • Fullscreen styling. A popover's UA default is fit-content with auto margins; inset: 0; margin: 0; width: 100vw; height: 100dvh makes it an app shell. Entry/exit animate with @starting-style + transition-behavior: allow-discrete.
    • Microdata stays whole. Moving recipeInstructions out of the card and into the popover would normally break the Recipe item — itemref="app-steps" on the card's itemscope folds the HowToStep list back in. Validate with /validate-schema.

    What needs JavaScript — and its caveats

    • Screen Wake Lock (navigator.wakeLock): secure context only; the lock is released by the platform whenever the tab is hidden, so it must be re-requested on visibilitychange. The indicator dot reflects the sentinel's real state (its release event), not the toggle.
    • Read-aloud, two ways. speechSynthesis is free and per-step (dynamic text), but voice quality and voice loading are inconsistent across platforms. A pre-recorded audio file gives production quality and works where TTS voices are poor — but is static content (here a placeholder file). Playing one cancels the other.
    • Hands-free (SpeechRecognition): Chromium-only in practice (webkit-prefixed), needs mic permission, and on most builds streams audio to a server. Recognition auto-stops on silence, so it must be restarted from onend; it is also paused while the app itself is speaking so TTS doesn't trigger commands. Keyboard arrows do the same job as fallback.
    • Next steps if this graduates from demo to component: per-step timers from HowToStep durations, a card ↔ app View Transition morph (lightbox.js pattern), Media Session metadata for the narration, and moving the inline script into an optional recipe.js module.
