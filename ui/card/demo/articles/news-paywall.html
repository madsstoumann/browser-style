Who pays for the carbon tariff — and who keeps the rebate

Jonas RahbekEconomics editorJul 2, 20266 min read

The tariff's headline rate was the easy part. What decided the negotiation was the rebate: exporters recover tariff costs against verified decarbonisation investment, which means the mechanism's real cost lands on whoever cannot prove they invested.

Our sector-by-sector model, built from the coalition's own working papers, puts steel and cement in line for rebates worth more than they pay in — and leaves agriculture, which has no comparable capital programme, carrying the net bill.

The numbers are not small. On the working papers' central scenario the steel sector recovers 112% of its tariff exposure by year three, cement 104%, chemicals 97%. Agriculture, with no decarbonisation ledger to draw on, recovers 9% — and pays the balance through input costs it cannot pass on.
