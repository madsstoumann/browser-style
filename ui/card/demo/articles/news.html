Coalition agrees on sweeping climate package

TB Thomas BergNewsroomJul 1, 20263 min read

After fourteen months of negotiation — and two near-collapses of the governing coalition — party leaders emerged at dawn with a climate agreement broader than anything previously attempted.

The package rests on two pillars. A carbon border tariff, phased in over three years, will price imported goods by their embedded emissions, aligning trade policy with the domestic carbon levy for the first time. And a national retrofit programme will fund insulation, heat pumps and rooftop solar for an estimated 1.4 million homes, prioritised by energy poverty rather than postcode lottery.

Opposition to the tariff came, until the final week, from the coalition's own industry wing. The breakthrough was a rebate mechanism: exporters recover tariff costs against verified decarbonisation investments. “We stopped arguing about whether to price carbon and started arguing about who keeps the receipts,” one negotiator said.

Economists broadly welcomed the deal, though several warned the retrofit programme's success depends on a construction workforce that does not yet exist — the plan includes funding for 40,000 apprenticeships, but the first cohort will not be certified before the programme's second year.

Parliament votes on the package within the month. With the coalition's combined majority, passage is considered near-certain; implementation begins January 1.
